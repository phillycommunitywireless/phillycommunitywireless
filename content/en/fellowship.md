## Public Internet Fellowship: Call for Proposals

A program of Philly Community Wireless

## About the Fellowship

Philly Community Wireless (PCW) operates free, no-cost public Wi-Fi across Norris Square, Fairhill, and Kensington: a community-controlled network with hundreds of access points and over 7,600 unique users a month. The Public Internet Fellowship invites local artists, technologists, and residents to use our network as a platform for community media, supporting new ways of empowering communities to come together in shared spaces to use the internet to better their lives and spaces.

Through this year's Fellowship program, we seek to foster collaborations between community organizations stewarding public green spaces with local artists, technologists, and residents to create and install interactive art, environmental resources, and previously unimagined forms of community digital storytelling that bring people together in a shared space and give voice to their diverse perspectives through access to the internet.

Preference will be given to projects that reflect the unique character of individual public spaces, activate modes of cultural resistance against forms of oppression, and/or expression of alternative visions for community futures.

## Project tracks

Projects fall into one of two descriptive tracks:

**Practical track**: Civic-information projects designed for everyday neighborhood use. Examples: bus arrival displays for SEPTA stops; multilingual heat, weather, or air-quality alert systems; community payment terminals; environmental sensors mounted in public space with public-facing readouts; local civic-information dashboards.

**Creative track**: Larger media installations with bigger production needs. Examples: augmented-reality neighborhood storytelling; outdoor light sculptures responsive to network or environmental data; location-based interactive media; outdoor screening setups for community film and audio; multilingual oral-history installations.

The mix of practical and creative projects in the pilot cohort will be determined by the Advisory Board based on the strength of applications received.

## Who can apply

* Individual artists, technologists, designers, organizers, or residents.
* Collaborative teams (apply jointly with one named lead).
* Applicants new to grant-funded work as well as established artists.
* You don't need to be incorporated, fiscally sponsored, or have 501(c)(3) status.

## What we provide

* Stipend of $10,000–$15,000, covering the Fellowship awardee's labor and materials.
* Connection to a community partner organization that will host or co-host the installation.
* Free public Wi-Fi at the installation site.
* Technical support during installation, including hardware selection, mounting, weatherproofing, and electrical or solar power where needed.
* Participation in the showcase event that brings people together to celebrate the work.
